Chapter 1 · Lesson 16

Three Types of Muscle Tissue

Skeletal musclemy/o (muscle) + muscul/oVoluntary, striated muscle attached to bone via tendons. Controls all intentional movement. is voluntary, striated, and attached to bone via tendons — it controls every intentional movement. Smooth muscleleiomy/o (smooth muscle)Involuntary, non-striated. Lines hollow organs — blood vessels, GI tract, bladder. lines hollow organs and is involuntary, controlled by the autonomic nervous system. Cardiac muscle — found only in the myocardium — is involuntary, striated, and uniquely autorhythmic, generating its own electrical impulse. After myocardial infarction, cardiac muscle cannot regenerate.

Atrophy vs. Hypertrophy

Atrophya- (without) + troph/o (nourishment)Muscle wasting from disuse, immobilization, or denervation. Measurable as circumference difference. is muscle wasting — after casting, nerve injury, or prolonged bed rest. Hypertrophyhyper- (excessive) + troph/o (growth)Increase in muscle fiber size from resistance training. Normal adaptive response — not pathological. is the adaptive increase in fiber size from resistance loading. Document atrophy as: "Right quadriceps atrophy noted — 3cm circumference deficit compared to contralateral limb."

Other Key ConditionsTendinitis (tendin/o + -itis) — tendon inflammation from overuse. Fasciitis (fasci/o + -itis) — fascia inflammation (plantar fasciitis = heel pain). Contracture — permanent muscle shortening from immobilization. SITS — rotator cuff: Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres minor, Subscapularis.

Chapter 3 · Lesson 17 — Goniometer Lab

ROM Assessment: AROM, PROM, and the Goniometer

AROMActive Range of MotionROM the patient produces using their own muscles. Tests motor function and pain inhibition. is movement the patient produces with their own muscles. PROMPassive Range of MotionROM produced by the clinician moving the patient's limb. Tests structural joint integrity. is movement produced by the clinician. When PROM > AROM, the joint can structurally move but the patient can't generate the motion — indicating motor weakness or pain inhibition. A goniometerGreek: gonia (angle) + metr/o (measure)Clinical instrument to measure joint angle in degrees. Axis at joint center, arms along proximal and distal segments. measures angle in degrees: axis at joint center, stationary arm along the proximal segment, moving arm along the distal segment. Always compare bilaterally and document deficits explicitly: "12° dorsiflexion deficit right vs. left (8° vs. 20°)."

Sports Medicine Clinic · ROM Assessment · Follow-Up Visit

Patient: Jordan K., 16 — Basketball Ankle Sprain, Week 2 Follow-Up

Jordan returns two weeks after a right lateral ankle sprain. Non-weight-bearing and in an air cast since injury. Ankle feels stiff; heel is tight going upstairs. No swelling today. Neurovascular intact bilaterally. You perform a bilateral goniometer ROM assessment.

MotionRight (Injured)Left (Uninjured)Finding
Dorsiflexion AROM8°20°12° deficit
Dorsiflexion PROM14°22°8° deficit
Plantarflexion AROM38°48°10° deficit
Plantarflexion PROM44°50°6° deficit
Inversion AROM15°30°15° deficit
Eversion AROM8°15°7° deficit
Task 1 · Name the Muscles
Jordan's dorsiflexion and plantarflexion are both limited. Name the specific muscles from this week's muscle list that are responsible for EACH of these motions at the ankle. For each muscle, state its action and explain why it is affected by an ankle sprain and subsequent immobilization.
Dorsiflexion (foot up toward shin): Tibialis Anterior is the primary dorsiflexor. Plantarflexion (foot down, tiptoe): Gastrocnemius and Soleus are the primary plantarflexors — the Achilles tendon connects both to the calcaneus. The Tibialis Posterior assists plantarflexion and also inverts the foot. After immobilization, all of these undergo disuse atrophy.
Task 2 · AROM vs. PROM Interpretation
For dorsiflexion: AROM is 8°, PROM is 14° on the right. The gap is 6°. What does this gap tell you clinically? What is the most likely explanation? Use correct terminology from your reading.
PROM > AROM = the joint can structurally move (PROM proves this) but the patient cannot generate that motion actively. This indicates motor weakness, pain inhibition, or neuromuscular deficit — NOT a structural block like contracture. If both were equally limited, you'd suspect a structural issue.
Task 3 · Atrophy Documentation
You observe Jordan's right calf appears visibly smaller. You measure: right calf 30cm, left 33cm. Name the specific calf muscles that have undergone this change, document the finding using the correct clinical term and root word breakdown, and explain the physiological reason.
The calf is composed of the Gastrocnemius (superficial, two heads) and Soleus (deep). Term: atrophy. a- (without) + troph/o (nourishment). Document: "Gastrocnemius/soleus muscle atrophy noted right lower extremity — 3cm circumference deficit compared to contralateral limb." Physiology: non-weight-bearing reduces motor nerve signals, protein synthesis slows, fiber cross-sectional area decreases within 48–72 hours of immobilization.
Task 4 · SOAP Objective Section
Write the complete Objective section of the SOAP note for Jordan K. Include all vitals (assume normal for a healthy 16yo), the full bilateral ROM table in clinical documentation format with deficits stated explicitly, and your muscle atrophy finding. Use at least 6 Week 6 terms correctly.
Key terms to include: AROM, PROM, goniometer, ROM deficit, bilateral, dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, atrophy with circumference measurement, gastrocnemius/soleus muscle names. Format: objective data only — no interpretation in the O section. Interpretation goes in Assessment (A).
